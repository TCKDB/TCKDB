# Hold literature-reported pressure-dependent kinetics

A set of phenomenological k(T,P) may be attached to a network either because the master equation was solved in this database or because the values were transcribed from a publication, and `network_solve.kind` says which. The `computed` member is the default and the preferred form, and every coverage rule that applied before this decision still applies to it unchanged. The `reported` member is not held to the state-energy, channel-barrier or energy-transfer coverage rules, requires `literature_id` instead, and raises the `reported_network_solve` upload warning.

Until now published k(T,P) could not be deposited at all. The chain was structural rather than deliberate: `network_kinetics.solve_id` is NOT NULL, `channel_kinetics` is reachable only through a solve, and a solve triggers three coverage rules — exactly one energy per network state, exactly one barrier per saddle-point path, and a collisional energy-transfer model for every well. Each of those rules is right for a solve run here; each asks for something that a paper's supplementary table, by construction, does not contain. A depositor holding a PLOG or Chebyshev fit out of a combustion-kinetics paper — the ordinary currency of the field, and the form in which most pressure-dependent kinetics actually reach a mechanism — was refused outright. The refusal produced no better data, only less: the numbers went into somebody's private mechanism file instead, uncited and unversioned. This is the argument [0005](0005-record-environments-do-not-grade-them.md) made for accepting a `described` execution environment and [0009](0009-record-what-energy-transfer-was-specified-over.md) made for accepting a network-wide ⟨ΔE⟩down — a validation rule that can only be satisfied by data the depositor does not have does not improve the record, it deletes it.

The obvious alternative was to relax `network_kinetics.solve_id` to nullable and hang reported rates off the channel directly. It is the wrong shape for two reasons. It would duplicate the solve's literature / software / workflow-tool provenance triple onto `network_kinetics`, where it would immediately be free to disagree with the copy on the solve. More seriously, a NULL `solve_id` would carry two incompatible readings — "these rates were transcribed from a publication" and "the writer never linked the solve" — with nothing in the row to separate them. That is precisely the ambiguity [0009](0009-record-what-energy-transfer-was-specified-over.md) removed for energy-transfer scope and that `network_channel.mechanism` removed for channel attribution, reintroduced one table to the left. A scientific claim about a record is positively asserted, never inferred from an absence. `network_solve` already carries the provenance triple, including the nullable `literature_id` a reported deposit needs, so the token belongs there and no new nullable FK appears. A check constraint then makes `literature_id` mandatory for the `reported` member, so the relaxation is paid for at the database level rather than only at the API boundary.

Calling such a row a "solve" is a stretch, and worth naming rather than glossing. `network_solve` was never only the record of a numerical procedure: it is the provenance envelope for one coherent set of k(T,P) — the T/P range they are valid over, the software and workflow tool that produced them, the paper they are credited to, and the collection of channel rates that belong together. `kind` says what stands behind that envelope. Renaming the table to match would churn public refs already minted on the deployed database (`nsolve_…`), the `/scientific/network-solves` route, and every client mirror, in exchange for a better noun; the docstrings carry the meaning instead. The axis is also deliberately not `ScientificOriginKind`, which classifies how science was *produced*. A paper's k(T,P) is usually computed science — somebody solved a master equation for it. What `reported` records is not that the numbers are non-computational but that *this database does not hold their derivation*, which is a different question with a different answer.

Under [0008](0008-validation-tiers-definitions-block-expectations-warn.md) the tiers follow. Published k(T,P) is correct, common, citable science, so a check that refused it could fire on a correct result and must not block; the completeness limitation warns instead. What stays definitional still blocks: a `reported` solve with no literature would assert rates carrying neither a derivation nor a source, which is weaker than either form this decision meant to admit, and a `reported` solve with no `channel_kinetics` reports nothing and contradicts its own kind. Relaxed also means *not required*, never *unvalidated* — a reported solve that volunteers a bath-gas composition or a state energy is held to exactly the same referential and physical rules as a computed one. And nothing was loosened on the computed side: the two list fields that stopped being `min_length` constraints became conditions on the `computed` member, because a schema-level `min_length` cannot see the discriminator.

What a reader should conclude from a `reported` record is narrow and specific: these rate constants are what the cited publication printed, and this database holds no part of the calculation behind them. Three things are therefore impossible that a computed record permits — the rates cannot be re-derived, the fit cannot be checked against the underlying microcanonical data, and the network cannot be re-solved at temperatures or pressures outside the reported range. The values are as trustworthy as their source; what is missing is the derivation, not the accuracy. Two solves of the same network, one computed and one reported, are not equally informative, and the warning exists so that a consumer comparing them is told so rather than having to notice. For the same reason it surfaces on every read path that reaches a rate, not only on the solve itself: on the solve detail and search records, on the solve summary embedded in a network, on the parent-solve context of a network-kinetics record, and on the compact kinetics projection embedded in a network. A consumer pulling rates must be able to tell the two apart without a second request and a join, because a reported record that read back indistinguishably from a computed one would be worse than refusing the deposit it came from. `computed` remains the preferred deposit; this decision admits a weaker record, it does not declare the two equivalent.
