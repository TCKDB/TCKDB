# Record what collisional energy transfer was specified over

A pressure-dependent upload may declare its ⟨ΔE⟩down either per (well, collider) pair or once for the whole network, and `network_solve_energy_transfer.scope` says which was declared. The per-well form remains preferred and is the default; the network-wide form is accepted, carries `state_id` and `collider_species_entry_id` as NULL *by declaration*, and raises the `network_wide_energy_transfer_scope` upload warning. Mixing the two in one payload is refused.

The physics behind the old rule is not in dispute. Collisional stabilisation in a master equation is a property of a specific well colliding with a specific bath partner: the average energy removed per downward collision depends on the density of states of the excited well and on the collision partner's ability to accept internal energy, so argon and helium do not relax the same well identically, and a tightly bound isomer does not relax like a floppy one. A five-well network in an argon bath therefore has five ⟨ΔE⟩down values in principle, and the validator that demanded exactly one entry per (well × bath collider) pair was asserting exactly that. It was scientifically defensible on its own terms.

It was nevertheless wrong in practice, because it confused what the quantity *is* with what a calculation *determined*. Arkane, RMG and MESS inputs routinely specify a single `SingleExponentialDown` applied network-wide — not because the author believes all wells relax identically, but because ⟨ΔE⟩down is rarely known well enough per well to be worth resolving, and a single value is the standard, publishable treatment. The old contract had no way to express that, so the only way to satisfy it was to paste one number once per well. The repository's own Arkane ingester did precisely this, with a note reading "Expanded from the run's single declared energyTransferModel". The resulting rows are indistinguishable from five independently determined values. That is a provenance loss *manufactured by the validation itself*, in a database whose central claim is that it records how a number was produced — and it is worse than the gap it was trying to close, because an absent value is honest while a duplicated one is a false positive that every downstream consumer will faithfully propagate. This is the same argument that made [0005](0005-record-environments-do-not-grade-them.md) accept a `described` execution environment rather than force a guessed digest.

Under [0008](0008-validation-tiers-definitions-block-expectations-warn.md) the tier follows immediately. The test is whether a check could fire on a correct novel result: a network-wide ⟨ΔE⟩down is correct, common, published science, so the check must not block. It is an expectation about resolution, not a definition, and expectations warn. What *is* definitional stays at the blocking tier — a `per_well` entry that names no well contradicts itself, a `network_wide` entry that names one contradicts itself, and a payload that mixes the two is genuinely ambiguous, since nothing in it says which wells the global entry covers or whether it overrides the specific ones. The error message's old premise, that "global transfer is scientifically ambiguous", was the one real mistake: a network-wide model is perfectly well defined. It is ambiguous only if recorded *as though* it were per-well, which is what the rule was forcing.

The scope is a stored token rather than an inferred one for the reason [0007](0007-curated-selections-are-a-release-overlay-not-a-column.md) and the `network_channel.mechanism` column already establish: a scientific claim must be positively asserted, never read out of an absence. Had `state_id` simply been made nullable, a NULL would have meant both "declared for the whole network" and "the producer dropped the field", with nothing in the row to separate them — precisely the ambiguity this decision exists to remove, relocated one column to the left. With the token present, a check constraint makes the two shapes mutually exclusive, so every NULL carries its own explanation. The enum leaves room for intermediate resolutions (per-well but not per-collider, as Arkane's per-species `energyTransferModel` actually expresses) without another change of shape; none is defined until a real producer needs one.

What a reader should conclude from a `network_wide` record is narrow and specific: one ⟨ΔE⟩down was determined and applied to every collisionally stabilised well and to the bath as a whole, and the well-to-well variation was **not determined** — not measured and found small, simply never resolved. The stored number is as trustworthy as its source; what is missing is resolution, not accuracy. Two solves of the same network, one per-well and one network-wide, are not equally informative, and the warning exists so that a consumer comparing them is told so rather than having to notice. Per-well remains the preferred deposit; this decision lowers a barrier to honest deposition, it does not declare the two forms equivalent.
